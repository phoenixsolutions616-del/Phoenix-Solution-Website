What an EICR is, and what it checks.

An Electrical Installation Condition Report, often called an electrical safety certificate, is a formal inspection and test of a property's fixed wiring. It covers the consumer unit, every circuit, sockets and switches, light fittings, earthing and bonding, and records anything that falls short of BS 7671, the UK wiring regulations.

Each issue is given a code. C1 means danger is present and we make it safe on the spot. C2 means potentially dangerous. FI means further investigation is needed. Any of these make the report "unsatisfactory". C3 means improvement is recommended and doesn't affect the result.

  • Plain English: every observation photographed and explained, not just a code on a form.
  • Honest coding: we grade to the standard, never to create work.
  • Itemised remedials: a separate fixed quote for anything that needs fixing, which you're free to take elsewhere.
  • Fast reissue: once remedials are done, a satisfactory certificate is usually reissued within 24 hours.

Who needs an EICR, and how often.

Landlords

A legal requirement for private rented homes in England: a new EICR at least every five years, a copy to tenants within 28 days, and any remedial work finished within 28 days of an unsatisfactory report. Councils can fine up to £30,000 per breach.

Homeowners & buyers

Recommended every ten years, when you buy a property, or before major work. A pre-purchase EICR can save a sale: we've turned around remedials and a satisfactory certificate in three days before exchange.

Businesses

Periodic inspection supports your duties under the Electricity at Work Regulations 1989, typically every five years, and it's the certificate your insurer asks for after an incident. Commercial EICRs from £250, priced by circuit count.
