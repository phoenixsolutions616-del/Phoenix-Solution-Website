Signs your fuse box needs replacing.

"Fuse box" and "consumer unit" mean the same thing: the board where your electricity supply is split into circuits. The difference is age. Older boards were built before the safety devices that now protect people from electric shock and homes from electrical fire, and many are still in service decades later.

  • Rewirable fuses: fuse wire in ceramic carriers, or a board you have to open with a screwdriver to reset.
  • No RCD protection: no "test" button, or a single RCD that takes the whole house off when one appliance faults.
  • Wooden backboard or plastic case: boards in homes now have to be non-combustible, usually steel.
  • Constant tripping: nuisance trips, warm or buzzing switches, or scorch marks around the board.
  • An EICR flagged it: a C2 or C1 code against the consumer unit means it needs attention to get a satisfactory report.
  • You're adding load: an EV charger, heat pump, battery or new kitchen often needs more ways and better protection.

What a Phoenix consumer unit upgrade includes.

We fit metal-cased consumer units with an individual RCBO on each circuit, so a fault on the kitchen sockets trips only the kitchen sockets. Every board includes a surge protection device (SPD) to protect the electronics in a modern home, and where the risk calls for it we add arc fault detection (AFDD).

  • Testing first: we test your existing circuits before the new board goes on, so hidden faults are found now rather than tripping the new RCBOs later.
  • Earthing & bonding: main earth and bonding to gas and water checked and brought up to standard.
  • Clear labelling: every circuit labelled properly, often for the first time.
  • Certified & notified: an Electrical Installation Certificate, and notification to Building Control as Part P requires.
  • Room to grow: spare ways planned for the EV charger, battery or heat pump you might add next.

How the day goes.

1. Photo survey

Send us photos of your fuse box and meter. We confirm a fixed price, usually within one working day.

2. Pre-testing

On the day we test your circuits before changing anything, and tell you about any faults before we go further.

3. Changeover

The old board comes out and the new one goes in. Power is off for part of the day and restored circuit by circuit.

4. Certificate

Final testing, labelling and your Electrical Installation Certificate, with Building Control notified for you.

Homeowners aren't required to replace a working board, but it's one of the most effective safety upgrades you can make. For landlords, a board that leads to a C1 or C2 code on an EICR must be put right within 28 days, and replacing it is often the simplest fix. Any replacement is notifiable work under Part P of the Building Regulations.
